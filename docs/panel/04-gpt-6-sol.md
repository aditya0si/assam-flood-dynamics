## 1. Current state, as built

Baanh is a static, versioned scenario explorer, not a flood forecast or historical-event reconstruction. The React app loads committed data, validates its contracts, and renders a MapLibre map, shared timeline and charts, district ranking, and methodology/provenance panels (`src/App.tsx:16–34`, `src/data/loadData.ts:102–171`, `src/map/flood.ts:32–39`, `src/charts/ChartGrid.tsx:34–35`, `src/panels/ProvenancePanel.tsx:21–43`). The pipeline fetches terrain, boundaries and mapped rivers, then thresholds HAND against a synthetic stage; it does not compute rainfall–runoff or route water (`pipeline/src/baanh_pipeline/hand.py:57–61`, `inundation.py:351–365`, `series.py:40–47`).

The committed contract has 12 steps, 90 m analysis cells and 180 m displayed flood frames (`public/data/manifest.json:15–26`; `public/data/flood/frame-index.json:12–18`). S07 is labelled “25 Aug,” but has no event year: its synthetic 3.7 m stage produces 10,010.6411 km² of modelled inundation and a 2.354 m median modelled depth (`public/data/flood/flood-summary.json:405–430`). The ranking covers 33 districts; Sivasagar leads at 71.944 on a score weighted 45% flooded-area share, 35% duration and 20% depth (`public/data/risk/district-risk.json:6–33`). These are scenario outputs, not measured flood or loss statistics.

For a portfolio, the unusually strong parts are executable data contracts, source-to-output provenance, labelled synthetic inputs, a usable blocked-tile fallback, and documented failures rather than silently adjusted results (`README.md:52–73, 95–130`; `docs/M0-REPORT.md:183–210`). The review record rejected an excessive original stage/channel mask, oversized vector frames, and misleading or flaky visual tests before the reported CI pass (`docs/REVIEW-02:17–38`; `REVIEW-04:25–38`; `REVIEW-11`–`14`). For disaster management, the weaknesses are decisive: no observed forcing, calibrated event, surveyed defence, operational update cycle or decision authority. Even the stated “conservative-shallow” depth convention is not a general safety bound: missing embankments could make extent too large in one place and missed breaches could make it too small elsewhere (`pipeline/config/scenario.json:42–68`). Passing internal plausibility gates is not external validation; the peak-area band was reviewer-selected (`public/data/flood/flood-summary.json:750–754`).

## 2. Gap analysis against a real disaster-management system

A district duty officer needs named gauges with datum and last-observed time; local warning thresholds; villages, roads, bridges and shelters with verified status; accessible routes; and a way to report a breach or close a road. A state emergency operations centre needs cross-district observations and forecasts, feed-health monitoring, uncertainty, escalation/approval records, and a common incident picture. An NDMA analyst needs comparable archived events, model-version history, validation by event and lead time, and auditable intervention comparisons. None is supplied by a 12-step static composite (`README.md:3–24, 95–109`; `docs/MILESTONES.md:374–380, 419–425`). The present “very_high” district class means high *modelled terrain exposure under this scenario*, not present danger, people affected or an instruction to deploy.

## 4. Simulation upgrade path

These are proposed engineering gates, **not achieved accuracy**. First, retain HAND as a clearly labelled 90 m screening baseline, add dated observed rain and *authorised* stage at named reaches, and test one real event without tuning to its peak. This buys an observed-context map cheaply; it still cannot produce defensible arrival times or defence effects. Compare event-timed Sentinel-1 wet/dry masks using precision, recall and intersection-over-union (IoU), reporting permanent water separately.

Next, model rainfall–runoff for gauged subcatchments at approximately hourly forcing and output; calibrate volume, peak discharge and peak timing on one event and hold out another. Provisional review targets: volume bias within ±20%, peak timing within 6 hours. Without permitted discharge records, call this an experiment, not a forecast. Add reach-specific, datum-controlled stage–discharge ratings and 1D unsteady routing; target held-out stage MAE ≤0.3 m and peak-time error ≤6 hours, revising those targets with gauge error and local expertise. HEC-RAS guidance explicitly calls for calibration against stage/flow hydrographs and verification on different events. ([hec.usace.army.mil](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/cwmsdocs/rasum/6.6/calibration-of-unsteady-flow-models-154864072.html?utm_source=openai))

Only for surveyed priority reaches, couple 1D channels to 2D floodplain cells initially around 30–100 m, refine critical controls locally, try 30–120-second computation steps subject to stability, and publish 15–60-minute outputs. This buys velocities, arrival times, backwater and overtopping at substantial survey, compute and QA cost; test held-out SAR IoU ≥0.65, stage MAE ≤0.3 m and surveyed high-water-mark absolute error ≤0.5 m as *candidate acceptance thresholds*, stratified by event and reach. Those thresholds cannot be certified with today’s data. Breaches require surveyed crest/condition and explicit location, width, formation-time and trigger ranges—not a “breach hotspot” inferred from HAND. Run ensemble rainfall, roughness, boundary and breach variants and show exceedance frequency plus where members disagree, never a single confident edge. HEC-RAS supports connected levee/2D-area breach studies, but its mechanism does not supply the missing local parameters. ([hec.usace.army.mil](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/cwmsdocs/rasum/6.2/levee-overtopping-and-breaching-114197629.html?utm_source=openai))

## 5. Prevention and mitigation features

A planning workspace should compare a frozen baseline with one changed intervention, showing depth, duration, people potentially exposed, access and uncertainty—not just a moved polygon. A WRD engineer could vary *surveyed* embankment crest/repair and breach assumptions; a drainage engineer could test mapped sluice/pump capacity or retention-area storage; a river engineer could test dredging only after repeated bathymetry and sediment surveys. Without those records, each control remains a labelled hypothetical, not an estimated benefit. WorldCover can support coarse land-cover/runoff alternatives, not parcel-level policy claims. ([waterresources.assam.gov.in](https://waterresources.assam.gov.in/information-services/detail/assam-river-atlas?utm_source=openai))

For warning, an authorised CWC/agency operator would map observed levels to approved local thresholds and display source time, crossing confidence and measured lead time from historical events; Baanh must not issue warnings itself. For evacuation, a district officer needs verified shelter locations/capacities, road/bridge status and accessible routing; OSM supplies a draft network only. Mark unverified routes “unknown,” never “safe.” ([cwc.gov.in](https://www.cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/hddp2018_0.pdf))

## 7. Non-negotiables

Keep synthetic dates and observations unmistakably separate; never turn the 4,000–12,000 km² internal plausibility band into a skill claim. Preserve native resolution, vertical datum, acquisition time, licence and source version. Do not publish classified CWC series or restricted NRSC products merely because a map is viewable. Expose missing sensors and stale or contradictory inputs rather than interpolating silently. Verify forecasts by independent events and lead time, including misses, not only attractive peak maps. Keep sensitive facility and evacuation information access-controlled where appropriate. An accountable authority—not this public website—must approve thresholds, warnings and actions; retain audit logs, an offline procedure and an explicit route back to official advice. ([cwc.gov.in](https://www.cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/hddp2018_0.pdf))
