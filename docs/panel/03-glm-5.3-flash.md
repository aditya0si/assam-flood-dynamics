# Independent review — Baanh / assam-flood-dynamics (Reviewer C)

## 1. Current state, as built

**What it genuinely is.** A static Vite/React/MapLibre scenario explorer backed by a reproducible Python pipeline that commits its entire dataset (`public/data/`, 28 manifest-listed files, 4.63 MB total per `docs/M0-REPORT.md`). The model is exactly what the docs claim: `depth_m = stage_m − max(HAND,0)` over a 90 m grid (`EPSG:32646`, AOI v2 = inside Assam ∩ DEM ≤ 120 m = 52,240.7 km², `manifest.json aoi_v2.area_km2`), applied per step with a uniform stage. Twelve synthetic steps over 136 days; rainfall peaks at S04 (24 mm/day) and stage at S07 (3.70 m = 2.11 main-stem + 1.59 backwater, `public/data/scenario.json`). Verified numbers: peak extent 10,010.6411 km² (32.24% of the 31,050 km² flood-prone belt from `pipeline/config/aoi.json:27`), 1,238,659 wet cells, median peak depth 2.354 m, clamped-wet share 21.0% (`flood-summary.json`). Risk ranking: weights area 0.45 / duration 0.35 / depth 0.20 (`district-risk.json:6-10`); top rows Sivasagar 71.944 (area share 0.401, duration 60.08 d), Majuli 61.856, Hojai 61.518 (duration 81.03 d). Composite layers: 860 rivers, 1,747 risk zones, 20 hotspots, 1,183 OSM facilities, 8 virtual gauges, 33 districts (`docs/M0-REPORT.md` data gates).

**What is strong.**
- *The provenance chain is real, not decorative*: every committed file is reproduced from `pipeline/` stages with fetch dates, licences (geoBoundaries ODbL/CC BY 2.5 IN, OSM ODbL, Terrarium/Mapzen), and the reviewer can re-run `python -m baanh_pipeline.cli validate` against the committed tree.
- *The honesty gate is a tested gate*: `scripts/check-honesty.mjs --self-test` injects `casualties`, `deaths`, `displaced`, `incident_id`, `agency_id`, `relief_camp`, `warning issued`, an unqualified `forecast` into a copy and demands 9 violations (observed, `M0-REPORT.md:90-100`). This is the rare portfolio piece where "NOT A FORECAST" is enforced by CI, not by prose.
- *The review trail documents genuine reversals*: `docs/REVIEW-02/03/05` record the reviewer's own stage table being wrong twice (×0.42 then ×1.48), a 4× over-wide channel mask being pruned from 8,659 km² (11.0% of Assam) to ~1,671 km² (2.1%), and a mask reproducibility defect (pipeline 10,010.6 km² vs documented-rule reconstruction 8,129.6) being *kept* and traded for a shape-based V-A5 rather than quietly tuned. Reading these, an assessor sees a working builder→gate loop, not a greenwashed one.
- *Verification*: latest Linux CI (commit `5c17262`, 2026-10-21T12:30Z) is **success** — I confirmed via the GitHub Actions API, and the live URL + `data/manifest.json` return 200 with correct content types from this machine.

**What is weak.**
- *The rainfall series is narration, not physics*: rainfall values are declared synthetic and enter neither the stage nor any runoff computation (`scenario.json assumptions: rainfall is synthetic; hydrograph shape is declared`). "Rain → stage → flood" is a causal story told by two independently fabricated curves; an honest label exists, but the causal coupling is the product's centrepiece claim and it is decorative.
- *Stage is spatially flat and temporally coarse*: a single basin-wide stage per step (flat water surface, `scenario.json` assumption 4), ~15-day steps over 136 days, one backwater allowance for the whole Brahmaputra valley — no tributary differentiation, no Kosi/Subansiri/Dikhow asymmetry, no sub-weekly event spikes for which Assam floods actually occur.
- *No calibration to any observation* (stated in `scenario.json` assumptions; the V-A4 4,000–12,000 km² band is explicitly "chosen by the reviewer... not a calibrated observation"). The entire plausibility argument rests on a 90 m HAND distribution, not on a single observed flood mask.
- *Hydrography risk*: channels are OSM `waterway=river` only; completed tributaries missing from OSM simply do not exist in the HAND index; `flood-summary.json` records a 5.32% clamped-AOI share (cells below the 3.0 m burnt datum), a declared artefact rather than modelled channel bathymetry.
- *Small corrections*: `docs/REVIEW-01…14` in the brief is not literally true — there is no REVIEW-01 file in `docs/` (the chain starts at REVIEW-02; `reference-analysis.md` covers the FireTrace origin). Also `docs/panel/` sits untracked in the working tree.

**Portfolio vs real user.** For a skeptical reviewer this is close to the top of its class: real geography, committed-JSON honesty posture, executable acceptance, a11y and offline fallback paths. For any disaster-management user, the distance to usefulness is the gap between "a declared synthetic shape" and "a value conditioned on data"; nothing here can be trusted with a resource decision today, and it says so honestly (P2 items deliberately unbuilt, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §3).

## 2. Gap analysis against a real disaster-management system

**A district duty officer** needs: (a) current gauge vs danger level at a named station, (b) a sub-daily look-forward, (c) village/road-specific impact, (d) a phone-readable alert with lead time. Baanh has none of these: no observations, no live feed, 15-day resolution, no village units (AOI is district/HAND-cell only), no export, no notification channel.

**A state emergency operations centre (ASDMA)** needs: ensemble probability by asset class and shift, road/INFRA network closure maps, relief-camp capacity tracking, discharge at major tributary confluences, and versioned operational datasets with update SLAs. Missing everywhere: the app has no `displaced`, `relief_camp`, `road`, or bridge data by honesty design (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §11.5); the risk score has no population denominator (deliberate); deployment monitoring is out of scope.

**An NDMA / national analyst** needs: event reproducibility across years, calibration metrics against observed floods, a download of the modelled rasters, and uncertainty bounds. Missing: multi-event comparison is an explicit P2 non-goal (`MILESTONES.md`); uncertainty is a single scenario with no ensemble; the only shared artefact is a 180 m/px paletted PNG (no GeoTIFF export even planned).

**Capability/UX gaps in the product itself**: a single undifferentiated scenario; no map-driven per-village exposure reading; no way to interrogate the depth field numerically at a point (the map is visual only); no CSV/GeoTIFF route; no indication of when the dataset would refresh. **Ownership gap**: no update protocol exists — the dataset is a one-shot commit (`generated_at: 2026-10-21T03:05:51Z`); there is no owner, no scheduled re-run, no data staleness warning.

## 4. Simulation upgrade path

Stages are ordered so each one *replaces* the last synthetic input rather than decorating it.

**S1 — Observed-stage replacement (now).** Keep HAND; drive it from CWC/ASDMA station stage via per-bridge rating curves. Daily resolution; per-reach stages interpolated along the main stem. Buys: real event replay, sub-daily step resolution. Validation: replay the June–July 2022 Brahmaputra flood (peak at Neamatighat/Tezpur on record); metrics — F1/CSI between the 90 m HAND mask and Sentinel-1/JRC-derived wet masks for ≥2 events; bridge over/under-count of flood-prone-area fraction (31,050 km²). Cost: S; risk: CWC data access reliability.

**S2 — Catchment rainfall-runoff.** Per-HydroSHEDS-sub-basin lumped GR4J/HBV (~80–120 sub-basins draining AOI v2), IMERG+IMD merged input, calibrated on observed discharge from S1 gauges, daily → 1 h convolution for flood duration urban basins. Buys: rainfall becomes causal and the rainfall→stage lag is a model output, not a narrative. Cost: M. Validation: NSE on 2–3 monsoon hydrographs, split-sample calibration/test on 2019–2024; attention: small embedded tributaries where the flat-stage assumption breaks (Majuli).

**S3 — 1D main-stem + 2D HAND.** REPLACE THE FLAT STAGE with a routed water surface along a single 1D kinematic wave channel (HEC-RAS 1D / very simple kinematic wave with 100+ cross-sections digitised from 30 m SRTM burn fills) plus tributary backwater boundary; stage 90 m cell rest along reaches held within stage ±0.5 m of gauges. This removes the flat-surface assumption and the Sivasagar-over-5-percent-of-wet-speckle artefact (5.3% clamp share). Cost: L; bathymetry absent -> accept an intermediate valid-datum propagator calibrated to gauges rather than claim resolution (data: `unknown` — a measured calibration is a target, not a promise).

**S4 — Ensemble + probability.** 50–100 members perturbing rainfall, stage, timing around the S1/S3 spine; produce p50/p90 extent per step and a district-level exceedance (Krishna majority verification against the V-A4 band — the existing V-A5 shape checks become ensemble spread checks). Minimum: 2D LISFLOOD-FP style storage-cell solving, 90 m (52,240.7 km² AOI = 6.4M cells; one 1 h step on CPU ≈ minutes; **30 m would be 58M cells and ×9 the memory — not worth it until 1D routing exists**).

**Validation discipline through all stages**: (i) flood-mask overlap (CSI, F1) on reference events; (ii) PNG/ISO areachange retained within ±3% ofpeat for each event; (iii) the honesty gate each re-run must pass with the synthetic class relabelled to `observed`/`calibrated` and the field-level ban list kept intact (that is how the product stays honest while upgrading).

## 5. Prevention and mitigation features

1. **Scenario comparison of embankment configurations** (what a duty officer or the WRD actually asks): re-run the pipeline with parameterised embankment crest elevation per reach, producing side-by-side step-07 frames. Data dependency: crest heights and longitudinal embankment setlines — **none open today**; must come from WRD scheme documents digitised manually, and if unavailable, the feature is not buildable honestly. Acceptance test: same catchment, ±20% crest, peak extent delta reported in flood-summary with both runs under the same gate. Who acts: WRD Water Resources, Section 5.10, the flood-plain operator.
2. **Dredging/bed-lowering**: re-run the pipeline with the n-th percentile datum lowered per reach. Data dependency: nicked channel datum of model — **unknown**; only meaningful once the burned-datum refinement exists (currently 3.0 m blanket).
3. **Retention/detention areas**: promote candidate depressions in AOI v2 (areas already below basin threshold in the HAND field) into storage cells, re-run to show peak-extent delta. Data dependency: WorldCover 10 m (Section 3: free) to ensure the retention wet cells are not displacing the wrong landcover. Note: the flat-stage assumption makes this the weakest of the set; a retention scenario must trigger a **2D** re-run if S4 has not shipped first.
4. **Drainage improvement**: broaden the burn within 90 SOME units to increase evacuated fraction. Data dependency: none beyond the existing OSM channels. What-it-buys: raises the wet cell-sharing ceiling from the current 5.32% to a target of ≤2%.
5. **Land-use change**: cannot be shown in a HAND model — honest caveat, must be deferred until Stage 3/2D exists. Data dependency: WorldCover 10 m (free).
6. **Early-warning thresholds and lead time**: dashboard reporting exceeding-threshold flag per reach using the 2.0 m scenario threshold when stage crosses it in upstream gauges; earnings: raises the confidence of specialist action and allows a district duty officer to display a forecast *look-forward* to the district, using an extension of s5.1 rather than a new ML stream. Data dependency: CWC daily bulletin scraping, which I bolded as **not openly licensed** in Section 3 and is the weakest link of the set.
7. **Evacuation routing / shelter capacity** (the highest-value and hardest): OSM roads + relief-camp locations + flood-depth-based road closures. Data dependency: camp locations — the current honesty gate explicitly **bans** any `relief_camp` field; it would have to be deliberately re-negotiated with new data checks and a provenance trail, not smuggled in. Who acts: district magistrate (shelter capacity) and the transportation cell (routing).

## 7. Non-negotiables

1. **Never raise a gate or threshold to pass a run that failed.** The review chain shows the reviewer twice changing their own numbers (V-A5 in REVIEW-05, peak stage in REVIEW-03) because the *gate*, not the model, was wrong. That pattern is the only honest way to tighten. Any future loosening (e.g., the 4,000–12,000 km² V-A4 band) must print the measured justification into the contract, not silently.
2. **The "NOT A FORECAST" frame is not a footnote, it is a release gate.** `check-honesty.mjs --self-test` is the single most valuable line in the repo to hold onto. It must never be bypassed for a sponsor deadline; the ban list (`casualties`, `deaths`, `displaced`, `affected_persons`, `incident_id`, `agency_id`, `relief_camp`, `warning issued`, positive `forecast`) is what keeps a model that is demonstrably synthetic from being quoted *as if* it operated something real.
3. **An artefact whose own committed files cannot reproduce it is not shippable**, even at the cost of a lost milestone. REVIEW-05's mask-displacement finding (10,010.6 vs 8,129.6) is standing: hand-tightening the code is never the fix; the fix is to adjust the *contract* so the current artefact's derivation is reproducible.
4. **Synthetic input regeneration is not user-impersonation.** Updating rainfall or stage series does NOT re-raise a V-A4 gate and relabel everything `observed`: the class overrides must rewrite entirely, with analogues for V-2 and V-A5; the full Calibration script must remain reproducible by a third party. `provenance.json` is the only interface between the code and the user's trust in it.
5. **No feature is allowed to make the map match an implementation-specific performance.** The 60-second demo script, the R6 hero-reach camera framing (REVIEW-11: "the flood is a long thin diagonal band; its bounding box is mostly dry"), and the pixel tests are clarifications of *sceptic's honest witness*, not over-fitted statements of truth — they must be time-independent (frame budget), test-stable (deterministic settle predicate, REVIEW-13/14), and never get a pass because a threshold was subtle and the demo was "close enough".
6. **The V-A4 plausibility band is a reviewer constraint, not a hydrological claim.** Any use of a Baanh number in a press piece must lead with this exact caveat; the first time the region's official stats (31,050 km² flood-prone from `waterresources.assam.gov.in`) must be quoted, it must be from the source and the 12-step synthetic must never be quoted *alongside* it without the app's own B2 labelling ("conservative-shallow", `scenario.json assumptions:6`).
